# Relentless discovery interview

Adapted from the [bundled upstream sources](upstream.md). Pursue unresolved meaning and consequences within the user's scope; a long questionnaire or invented features cannot substitute for understanding.

## Follow the decision tree

Map outcomes to actors, journeys, capabilities, and decisions. Each answer unlocks dependent questions. Keep an overview of every major in-scope area while exploring one branch deeply; a simple app need not become an enterprise product.

Track branches as open, blocked by a named prerequisite, settled with evidence, explicitly deferred, or rejected. These are discovery-note states, not new prototype-schema values. Unknown does not mean unnecessary. A blocked branch remains unresolved even when no question is currently answerable.

After each response:

1. Capture what was actually answered. Agreement on one question or preview does not approve everything else.
2. Probe consequential ambiguity or contradictions with concrete scenarios; inspect effects on earlier decisions.
3. Research factual prerequisites yourself. Retain unverifiable facts as uncertainties and ask only for inaccessible context that matters.
4. Ask the next dependency-ready question or small group of independent questions, with recommendations and tradeoffs. Wait for answers before settling choices.

Ask one at a time when an answer determines the next question or the user prefers it. Keep larger sets in the branch record. Make clear which recommendation “yes” accepts; silence, timeouts, and your own recommendations are not answers.

For “I don't know,” offer a concrete example, meaningful alternatives, or a prototype experiment. For “you decide,” choose within that delegation, record why, and leave unrelated choices open. Do not ask again for a delegated routine decision.

## Expose missing needs

Trace each capability from entry through action, outcome, and what follows. Explore relevant rules, data ownership/lifetime, access, dependencies, discoverability, editing/cancellation/reversal, and empty/loading/failure/recovery states. Explain why each proposal follows from the goal; distinguish necessities from optional improvements. Deferral or removal may be better than adding features.

“Login” raises actors and sign-in method before password rules. “Settings” follows configurable behavior and its owner, rather than a generic menu. Use [discovery.md](discovery.md) to retain these distinctions and their evidence.

Reopen settled choices only when evidence or intent changes. A clear superseding user instruction is a new decision: record it and revisit affected dependents without redundant confirmation. Keep ambiguous implications open.

## Checkpoints and completion

A prototype can resolve an experiential question while other branches remain open. Follow [prototype.md](prototype.md) and return through [planning-handoff.md](planning-handoff.md). Continue independent branches in the main chat while the worker handles feedback; reconcile before editing shared records.

Before declaring completion, sweep the entire agreed scope, including unprototyped and blocked branches. Resolve, reject, or explicitly defer them; expose remaining assumptions and prototype limitations. Confirm shared understanding unless already confirmed. Never claim to have discovered every future requirement.

Finishing a preview is not that confirmation. A request to finalize can supply it when remaining assumptions are understood; avoid a redundant approval ceremony. An early plan request gets an honest draft. On pause, preserve the next branch; on stop, stop without one last question.
